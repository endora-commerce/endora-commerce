A consumer's withdrawal settles as a refund unless the consumer's consent to another resolution is on record (#905). A `return` filed by a personal Organization while the withdrawal is still possible for the order (the statutory 14 days, the channel's longer free-return window, or the 7-day grace after them) is classified as a consumer withdrawal when it is filed, read through `organizationDetailsPort`; the case detail reports `consumerWithdrawal`. Such a case can no longer be settled as credit, a replacement or a repair until an operator records the consumer's consent to that resolution: the settlement answers `422 RETURN_ALTERNATIVE_RESOLUTION_CONSENT_REQUIRED` with `details.resolution`. `POST /api/v1/admin/returns/:id/alternative-consent` (`returns:write`) records the consent (resolution, when, and an evidence note) and `DELETE` on the same path revokes it; both are Commands, audited as `return_case.alternative_consent.record` and `.revoke` without the note's text, and both answer `409` (`consent_frozen`) once the case is settled. The settlement prefill proposes a refund to the order's own payment method; a refund to another method is accepted and its `return.settled` audit entry records `refundMethodDiffers`. Company returns and complaints are unchanged, and cases filed before this release stay unclassified (`null`) and settle as before. A new migration adds the consumer-withdrawal columns to `return_cases` and creates `return_withdrawal_statements`, which nothing writes yet. The `returns.free_return_days` description now cites Directive 2011/83 art. 9 for the 14 days instead of Directive (EU) 2023/2673.
