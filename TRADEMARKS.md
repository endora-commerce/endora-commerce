# Trademarks

The code of Endora Commerce is open source under the MIT licence (`LICENSE`), and you may use,
copy, modify and redistribute it as that licence allows. **The MIT licence grants no rights in the
project's names or logos.** This page says how you may use them, so that a stranger can always
tell the project's own releases from somebody else's.

The names **Endora** and **Endora Commerce**, and the project's logos, identify software released
by Endora sp. z o.o. They are referred to below as *the marks*.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your work is built on or works with.** *"Built on Endora Commerce"*, *"a module for
  Endora Commerce"*, *"compatible with Endora Commerce 0.x"* and *"hosting for Endora Commerce"*
  are all fine, as long as nothing suggests that your product is the project's own or is endorsed
  by it.
- **Refer to the project** in articles, talks, tutorials, courses, comparisons and reviews,
  critical ones included.
- **Run and redistribute the unmodified project under its own name.** A copy of an
  official release, or a package installed from the `@endora-commerce` scope, is Endora Commerce.
- **Keep the name in the code.** Package names, identifiers, the `endora` command, and the notices
  and licence texts in the source stay as they are; you are not asked to rename anything to fork.

## What needs permission

- **Naming a fork or a derived product with the marks.** A modified distribution must not be
  called *Endora*, *Endora Commerce* or a confusingly similar name — including *"Endora Commerce
  Pro"* or a name that only adds a prefix or suffix. Call it something else and say that it is
  based on Endora Commerce.
- **Publishing a modified package under the `@endora-commerce` scope or another name that
  presents it as an official release.**
- **Using the marks in a product name, a company name, a domain name or a logo of your own**, or
  in a way that suggests the project sponsors, endorses or certifies you.

## Asking

Open an issue in this repository before you publish, describing what you would like to do. The
answer is recorded there, so the next person with the same question can read it.

This page describes a policy, not a licence, and it does not limit anything that trademark law
allows everybody to do anyway — naming a product truthfully in order to describe it is one of those
things.
